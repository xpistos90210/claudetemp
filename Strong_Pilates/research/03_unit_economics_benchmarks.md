## 1. Revenue benchmarks

### Network-implied average revenue per studio

From Mark Armstrong's March 2026 Authority Magazine interview, **STRONG Pilates global network revenue is ~US$50m across 100+ studios → ~US$500k per studio average ≈ £395k**.
([Source](https://medium.com/authority-magazine/mark-armstrong-of-strong-pilates-five-things-i-wish-i-knew-before-opening-a-franchise-136c52820888))

This is the network *mean*. It is dragged up by mature Australian sites (4–5 years trading, established member base, lower rent) and dragged down by year-1 sites. A new UK studio in year 1 would normally do **40–60% of this** (£160–£240k); year 2 **65–80%** (£260–£320k); year 3 onwards **90–110%** if it works at all.

### Independent UK boutique pilates benchmark

The Pilates Consultant blog (the most credible UK industry analyst I found) puts UK boutique pilates studios in the **£200k–£400k turnover** band, with **net profit margins ranging from a loss to ~20%**. Strong studios are at the larger end of the size spectrum (1,500–2,500 sq ft, 15+ beds) and so a successful Strong unit might top out at **£400k–£600k turnover**, but with proportionally higher fixed cost.
([Source](https://www.pilates-consultant.co.uk/blog/why-uk-investors-struggle-to-see-value-in-foreign-pilates-franchise-models))

### Members per studio

Network: 37,000 members / 100 studios = **~370 members per studio average**. This is *active* members (people who pay or use credits in a typical month). At an average revenue per member per month (ARPM) of £85–£110, 370 members → ~£380–£490k annual revenue, which is consistent with the global mean.
([Same source](https://medium.com/authority-magazine/mark-armstrong-of-strong-pilates-five-things-i-wish-i-knew-before-opening-a-franchise-136c52820888))

### Class capacity math

Strong Pilates studios run on **15+ Rowformer/Bikeformer beds**. Take 16 as a base. A typical schedule would be:
- 8 classes weekday × 5 days = 40 classes
- 5 classes Saturday + 4 classes Sunday = 9
- **Total: ~49 classes/week × 16 seats = 784 seats/week**
- Annualised: 784 × 52 ≈ **40,800 seats/year**

Realistic seat utilisation by maturity:
| Maturity | Utilisation | Seats sold/year | Implied revenue at £18 effective revenue/seat | Implied revenue at £25/seat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Months 1–3 | 25% | 10,200 | £184k annualised | £255k annualised |
| Months 4–12 | 40% | 16,300 | £294k | £408k |
| Year 2 | 55% | 22,400 | £404k | £560k |
| Year 3+ | 65% | 26,500 | £478k | £663k |
| Best case mature | 75% | 30,600 | £551k | £765k |

Effective revenue per seat depends heavily on **mix**: a member on an unlimited £180/month plan visiting 8 times pays £22.50/visit; a drop-in pays £28; a ClassPass user pays the studio £10–£14 net. Mixed effective revenue per seat for a healthy mature boutique reformer studio in suburban London is typically **£17–£22**.

## 2. Cost benchmarks

### Rent — Kingston upon Thames

The average asking rent for retail in Kingston Upon Thames is **~£30 per sq ft per annum** (Loopnet UK data, April 2026). Average available unit size 2,216 sq ft. Smallest 280, largest 28,000.
([Loopnet UK](https://www.loopnet.co.uk/search/retail-space/kingston-upon-thames-lnd--united-kingdom/for-rent/))

For comparison: Wimbledon retail averages ~£37 per sq ft. So Kingston is ~20% cheaper per sq ft for comparable retail/leisure space.

For a **2,000 sq ft studio at £30/sq ft = £60,000 rent/year**. Add **business rates** (typically 35–45% of rateable value, RV usually 60–80% of rent for retail) → **~£18–£22k/year**. Add **service charge** for shopping centre or managed unit → **£8–£14k/year**. Add **insurance** £3–5k.

**Total occupancy cost (rent + rates + service + insurance) for a 2,000 sq ft Kingston unit ≈ £90k–£100k/year (~£7,500–£8,300/month).**

A bigger 2,500 sq ft unit would push this to ~£110–£125k/year.

### Equipment

Strong's package quoted in Canada was **US$180k+ for the "Formers/Equipment/Merchandise/Tech Pack"** before tax and shipping/duties. That's roughly **£140–£155k** in GBP at April 2026 rates, before UK duties, VAT and import. Allow **£155–£180k all-in delivered to a UK studio** for the proprietary Rowformer + Bikeformer fleet of 15–18 beds, sound, screens, and HQ tech stack. This is materially more expensive per bed than generic Reformer kit (Align-Pilates / Balanced Body Allegro 2 at £2.5–4k per bed) — Strong's IP premium is real.
([Franchise Direct Canada via search](https://www.franchisedirectcanada.com/fitness-franchise-opportunities/strong-pilates))

### Build-out / fit-out

UK boutique fitness fit-outs run **£100–£200/sq ft** depending on the level of finish, AC, sound-proofing, showers, and changing-room density. For a 2,000 sq ft Kingston unit:
- Light fit-out (white box ready): ~£80–£120k
- Mid-range (typical Strong Pilates spec — sound-proofed, mood lighting, showers, changing rooms, branded reception): **£180–£280k**
- Premium fit-out (full Wimbledon Quarter spec): £280–£400k

### Staff

Three layers in a typical Strong Pilates studio:

1. **Instructors** — typically paid £30–£50 per class in suburban London, more for senior. A studio running 49 classes/week × £40 average = £1,960/week ≈ £102k/year. Plus on-costs (NI, holiday) ~13% → **~£115k/year**.
2. **Front desk / studio host** — 1.5 FTE at £25–£30k each → **~£40–£45k/year**.
3. **Studio manager** (if owner is not full-time onsite) — £35–£45k → **£40k**. If owner is full-time and not paying themselves a salary in year 1, this falls away (but you're then trading time for money).

**Total staff cost in a fully-staffed studio: ~£180–£200k/year** for an owner who isn't pulling a salary.

### Franchise fees (paid to HQ)

- **Royalty:** 8% of gross revenue
- **Brand fund / marketing levy:** assume 2% of gross (category norm; not specifically confirmed for Strong)
- **Total HQ take:** 10% of gross

On a £400k revenue studio: £40k/year going to HQ, *whether or not the studio makes a profit*.

### Equipment financing / depreciation

If the £165k equipment package is financed over 5 years at 10% APR: monthly cost ≈ **£3,500/month ≈ £42k/year**. If paid cash up front, the depreciation (5-year straight line) is £33k/year for accounting and a £165k cash hit on day one.

## 3. Time to break even

**Industry rule-of-thumb for boutique reformer pilates in the UK:**

- *Operating breakeven* (monthly revenue = monthly cash opex): typically **9–18 months from opening**, *if* the studio reaches viable utilisation
- *Cumulative breakeven* (recovery of all sunk capital): typically **3–5 years**, *if* the studio reaches viable utilisation
- **30–40% of new boutique fitness studios in the UK do not reach operating breakeven within 24 months** and either close or are sold at a loss to a successor operator. (This is a category-level statistic gathered from boutique fitness broker commentary; it is not Strong Pilates specific. Treat as directional.)

Sources directionally consistent on these ranges: [Pilates Consultant](https://www.pilates-consultant.co.uk/blog/strategic-financial-management-in-pilates-studios-the-profitable-vs-the-less-profitable), [Boutique Fitness Broker](https://boutiquefitnessbroker.com/blog/are-pilates-studios-profitable), [Gymdesk earnings guide](https://gymdesk.com/blog/how-much-do-pilates-studio-owners-make-a-realistic-earnings-guide).
