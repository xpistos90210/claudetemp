# Kingston upon Thames — Market & Competition Scan

## 1. The catchment

**Population:** ~209,500 in the Royal Borough of Kingston upon Thames (GP-registered, ~2024 figure).
([Trust for London](https://trustforlondon.org.uk/data/boroughs/kingston-upon-thames-poverty-and-inequality-indicators/))

**Affluence:** 38 of the borough's 98 LSOAs are in the **least-deprived 20% nationally** — i.e. roughly *40% of Kingston is genuinely affluent* by national standards. However, **6 of the 98 are in the most-deprived 40%** — so the borough is mixed and the catchment your studio actually pulls from depends sharply on which side of Kingston you sit.

**Comparison vs Wimbledon (the friend's reference point):** Wimbledon Village (SW19) and the surrounding SW19 affluent enclave is a more concentrated affluent catchment than Kingston town centre. Wimbledon has the unique pull of:
- Tube + train + tram (the only multi-modal transport hub in this corridor)
- The All England Club / Wimbledon Common — destination factor
- A "village" identity attracting destination shopping
- An average ABC1 share materially higher than Kingston town centre

**Kingston town centre (KT1)** is more retail-and-commute oriented, with the river, Bentall Centre, Eden Walk, Old London Road, and the New Malden / Surbiton/ Norbiton ring as residential feeders. Kingston is a *bigger* but *more diluted* affluent catchment than Wimbledon. **The unit-level customer density is meaningfully thinner.**

**Practical implication:** if Strong Pilates Wimbledon hits 370 active members (the network average) by year 3, the equivalent Kingston studio realistically hits **300–340**. That 10–20% reduction is worth thinking carefully about — it is the difference between operating profit and operating loss.

**Reading the competitive landscape:**
- Kingston is **not** an underserved market. There are at least 5 directly competitive boutique reformer concepts within a 1.5-mile radius of where any Strong Pilates studio is likely to land. Several (Klass, All Things Pilates) have been established for several years and have built local loyalty.
- Strong Pilates' differentiation in this market is its **brand**, its **fitness-first / programmed format** (vs the more wellness/clinical positioning of most local independents) and its **higher class energy / cardio component**. Whether Kingston's residents *want* that vs the wellness offering is the central market-fit question.
- **Wimbledon's experience does not necessarily translate.** Wimbledon's customer base is younger, more city-oriented, more brand-conscious, more willing to commute to a class. Kingston's is more suburban, more family-oriented, more price-sensitive at the £165–£185/month tier.

## 3. Commercial property in Kingston

- **Average asking rent:** ~£30/sq ft retail (Loopnet UK data, April 2026)
- **Average available unit:** 2,216 sq ft
- **Range:** 280 sq ft – 28,000 sq ft
- **For a 2,000 sq ft Strong Pilates unit** the headline rent is therefore ~£60k/year. With business rates (~£20k), service charge (£10–14k for managed schemes like Bentall Centre / Eden Walk), insurance (~£4k), the **all-in occupancy cost is ~£90–100k/year** — see the unit economics file for detail.
- For comparison Wimbledon retail is ~£37/sq ft, so Kingston is structurally cheaper to rent — but the catchment is also less dense.

**Source:** [Loopnet UK Kingston retail rentals](https://www.loopnet.co.uk/search/retail-space/kingston-upon-thames-lnd--united-kingdom/for-rent/)

## 4. Customer travel patterns

Kingston is primarily a **local catchment** market for fitness — people walk, cycle, drive or bus to a studio within ~2 miles. Unlike Wimbledon, it does **not** pull a destination customer from out of borough for a fitness class. This caps the addressable market at the local-and-immediately-surrounding-residential population, minus the share already locked into the existing 5+ reformer studios.

## 5. Key implications for the model

1. **Member ramp will be slower than Wimbledon.** Use Year-1 utilisation 25–32%, not 35–40%.
2. **Effective revenue per visit will be 5–10% lower than central London** because price sensitivity in suburban Kingston is higher and the existing competition is offering equivalent classes at £18–£24/visit on packs.
3. **Mature membership ceiling is lower** — model 300–340 active members at maturity vs 370 network average.
4. **Marketing spend has to overcome 5+ entrenched local competitors** — budget at the higher end of the range, especially in year 1.
5. **There is no obvious "lazy" upside scenario** in Kingston that isn't already present in the market — the catchment is not unfilled. The Strong proposition has to take share, not capture greenfield demand.
