### Notable patterns

- Of the four identifiable studio-level entities (rows 3, 4, 5 plus the original UK Ltd which also operated some sites), **two are formally dead** within 16–23 months of incorporation. **A 50% short-term mortality rate among the entities visible in Companies House search.** This is *not* the network mortality rate — but it is the mortality rate of the entities Companies House lets us see, and it is alarming.

- The cluster of December 2024 events is too synchronised to be random:
  - **7 December 2024** — a director of Strong Pilates UK Limited resigns
  - **17 December 2024** — Strong Pilates Battersea Ltd dissolved
  - **17 December 2024** — Strong Pilates Franchising UK Limited incorporated, registered c/o Stevens & Bolton LLP (a Guildford law firm with strong corporate restructuring practice), with one of the Australian co-founders listed
  - Subsequently — Strong Pilates UK Limited put into liquidation

- The new entity is registered at a **law firm address**, not a trading premises. This is normal for a brand-new SPV but tells us no operational team is yet attached to it in the public record.

- Most operating studios (Wimbledon, Fulham, Ealing, Warren Street, etc.) **do not appear under their own "Strong Pilates [location] Limited" name in Companies House search results**. This means franchisees are operating those studios through differently-named SPVs (often a personal-name LLP or a "Wellbeing", "Fitness", "Studio" suffixed company). Without paying the £15 to pull each filing we cannot definitively map every UK studio to its operating entity. Strong Pilates does not enforce a naming convention on its franchisees, which makes the public-record audit trail much harder.

## 2. Filings analysis — what the accounts tell us (and don't)

### Critical limitation

UK companies below the small-company thresholds (turnover under ~£10.2m, balance sheet under £5.1m, employees under 50) are entitled to file **filleted accounts** — these contain a balance sheet and minimal notes but **no profit and loss account, no turnover, no profit/loss figure, no director remuneration**. Almost every individual Strong Pilates studio company will fall in this regime.

What we *can* see:
- Net assets / shareholders' funds (a proxy — losses erode this)
- Cash at bank
- Creditors due within one year (trade and bank)
- Whether the auditors have signed off (small companies: usually unaudited)
- Charges (secured lending)
- Whether filings are on time

What we **cannot** see without paying for further documents or asking the franchisee directly:
- Revenue
- EBITDA / operating profit
- Salaries / drawings
- Membership counts

### Strong Pilates UK Limited (14353169) — the master franchisor

- **Last accounts:** to 31 December 2023 (the company's first accounting period). Next accounts (year ending 31 Dec 2024) were **due 30 September 2025** — they are now **overdue by ~6 months as of April 2026**.
- **Status:** Liquidation
- **Inference:** A liquidator is now in place; subsequent accounts may never be filed in the normal sense — the liquidator's progress reports replace them. The first 16 months of trading must have produced enough losses or working-capital pressure to push the entity into liquidation, *despite* a public narrative of "£10m network sales" and rapid expansion.
- **What to ask for**: the **liquidator's Statement of Affairs** (filed shortly after appointment) — this lists every creditor and what the company owes them. It is a public document and costs ~£15 per item from Companies House. **Get this**.

### Strong Pilates Battersea Ltd (15063351)

- Dissolved **17 December 2024** — the same day the new master franchisor entity was set up
- Incorporated 10 August 2023 — life of ~16 months
- This is consistent with either:
  - (a) a genuine business failure of the Battersea studio, with the SPV cleaned up post-failure, *or*
  - (b) a planned restructuring in which the Battersea studio was rolled into a different operating vehicle as part of the corporate reorganisation
- Without the dissolution paperwork (DS01 or strike-off notice in The Gazette) we cannot tell which. **Either reading is bad news for a prospective franchisee:** in case (a) a Strong Pilates studio in a prime SW London location with a strong demographic failed within ~14 months of opening; in case (b) the franchisor restructured itself in ways that reach down into the franchisees' own legal entities, which suggests the franchise agreement gives HQ unusually deep control.

### Strong Pilates (Market Harborough) Limited (15175472)

- Dissolved **26 August 2025**
- Incorporated 29 September 2023 — life of ~23 months
- Market Harborough is a small, affluent South Leicestershire town (~24,000 population). For a Strong Pilates studio — which needs a high-turnover, high-frequency boutique fitness customer base — a 24k-population town is an underwriting case study in why **catchment matters more than affluence**. If this is the same operator group as the still-live Strong Pilates (Leicester) Ltd — see below — it would suggest a multi-unit franchisee in the East Midlands tried two stores, killed the smaller one, and kept the bigger one.

### Strong Pilates (Leicester) Limited (14807288) — *the only individually-named studio entity still alive*

- Incorporated 17 April 2023 — about three years old
- Last accounts: year-end 30 April 2025
- Next accounts due: 31 January 2027
- **Status: Active**
- **Inference:** This is the most data-rich UK Strong Pilates franchisee entity in the public record. The accounts to 30 April 2025 cover its first full trading year (the studio likely opened in mid–late 2023). The filleted balance sheet — *if* the original report can be pulled (it requires a free Companies House account login but the document is public) — will tell us net assets, cash, creditors, fixed assets. This is the **single best public proxy for "what does a Strong Pilates franchisee actually look like 18 months in"**. **Pull this filing.**

## 3. Cross-checks

- **No charges** (secured loans) appear in the search results for the companies above. That is unusual — boutique fitness studios normally have an equipment finance lease and/or a bank charge. It may be the search snippet just doesn't surface them. **To be verified.**
- **No County Court Judgments** publicly visible in standard search snippets — but CCJs are filed at the Registry Trust, not Companies House, and a separate £6 search would be needed.
- **Persons of Significant Control**: Mark Francis Armstrong (Australian co-founder) is associated with the new Franchising UK Limited entity. This is consistent with Sydney HQ taking direct control of the UK after the December 2024 events. The previous structure was a UK master licence held by John Hastings; the new structure seems to be the Australian parent operating UK franchising directly through a UK SPV. **Whether John Hastings is still UK MD post-restructure cannot be verified from the search results.**

## 4. Bottom line for your friend

**The Companies House evidence does not prove that a Strong Pilates franchise will fail. But it does prove three things:**

1. The brand's UK corporate vehicle has *already* failed at the master franchisor level once (Strong Pilates UK Limited → liquidation), and the new vehicle is less than 18 months old.
2. At least two individual UK studio operating companies have closed their corporate doors within two years of incorporation.
3. The franchisor's public storytelling (50 studios in 3 years, £10m sales etc.) is meaningfully out of step with the public record (10–12 active sites; original UK entity dead; rollout running ~30%–40% of plan).

**A reasonable, neutral observer would, on this evidence alone, demand to see:**
- The liquidator's Statement of Affairs for 14353169
- Audited or accountant-prepared P&L for at least 3 UK studios that have been open >18 months
- A written explanation, signed by the franchisor, of (a) why Strong Pilates UK Limited went into liquidation, (b) what changed in the new franchise agreement, and (c) what protection a new franchisee has if Strong Pilates Franchising UK Limited follows the same path

**If the franchisor will not, in writing, answer those questions in detail, then *that* is the answer to whether your friend should sign.**
