# SBFIT Ltd (Wandsworth) & SBFIT Fulham Ltd — Filing Analysis

> Analysis of Companies House filings for the couple (Strahinja & Bojana Zarkovic)
> who own the Wandsworth Strong Pilates franchise and are about to open Fulham.
> These are the people reassuring our friend that the franchise is "a real success".

## Summary of findings

**The filed accounts for the Wandsworth entity (SBFIT Ltd, 15297896) cover only the
pre-opening period and show ZERO revenue. The studio did not open until Spring 2025.
There is no publicly verifiable evidence that Wandsworth is profitable — or even trading
at breakeven. The couple's verbal assurances to our friend are exactly that: verbal.**

### Critical observations

1. **ZERO REVENUE.** The notes state explicitly: *"The company did not generate any
   turnover during the period."* The entire 13-month period was pre-opening.

2. **£159 cash.** The company was running on fumes at year-end.

3. **£138,294 creditors due within one year.** Since the HSBC loan was not taken until
   March 2025 (after this balance sheet date), and there are no other visible funding
   sources, this is almost certainly the **directors' loan** — the Zarkovics' own money.

4. **Total capex on balance sheet: £125,456** (franchise fee £29,167 + equipment £96,289).
   - The franchise fee (£29,167) is **materially lower** than the ~US$50k–$59k quoted
     in franchise directories. Either the UK fee is lower, or this is a partial payment.
   - The equipment (£96,289) is **~60% of my modelled £165k**. Either fewer beds,
     or some equipment is leased off-balance-sheet, or a different pricing structure.

5. **Going concern note is boilerplate nonsense.** States *"good levels of sales growth
   and profitability"* for a company with zero turnover and negative net assets. This is
   a template pasted by the accountant. No evidential value.

6. **0 employees** for the entire period.

7. **Unaudited, unreviewed.** The accountant explicitly disclaims: *"We have not verified
   the accuracy or completeness of the accounting records."*

### What the next set of accounts will (and won't) show

The next accounts (year-end 31 Dec 2025) will cover the first ~9 months of actual
trading (Spring 2025 – Dec 2025). They are due for filing by approximately **September
2026**. However, as a small company filing filleted accounts, the P&L **will not be
delivered to the Registrar**. Only the balance sheet will be publicly visible.

What we *can* infer from the next balance sheet:
- Whether net assets have improved (trading profitably) or deteriorated (still losing)
- Whether the HSBC loan appears in creditors
- Whether the directors' loan has grown (more cash injected) or shrunk (being repaid)
- Cash position

What we *cannot* see: revenue, costs, profit/loss, membership numbers, utilisation.

## 3. HSBC Debenture — Fixed and Floating Charge

### Key facts
- **Date of charge:** 21 March 2025
- **Company:** SBFIT LTD (Wandsworth, 15297896)
- **Lender:** HSBC UK Bank plc
- **Type:** Fixed and floating charge over **ALL assets**
- **Contains:** Fixed charge(s), floating charge(s), **negative pledge**
- **Signed by:** Strahinja Zarkovic (Director), Bojana Zarkovic (Director/Secretary)
- **Bank Reference:** SC2579375716

### Critical terms

| Clause | What it means |
|---|---|
| **2.1 — Demand facility** | *"You will pay us, on demand, the Debt."* HSBC can call in the **entire loan at any time** for any reason. This is not a term loan. |
| **2.2 — Default interest** | Base rate + 3% above BoE base rate (currently ~7.5% total) |
| **3.1 — Security** | Covers literally everything: all land, fixtures, fittings, chattels, receivables, bank balances, goodwill, intellectual property, insurance rights, plus floating charge over all other assets |
| **5.1 — Negative pledge** | Company **cannot create any other security** without HSBC's written consent |
| **8.1 — Enforcement triggers** | Extremely broad: non-payment on demand, any breach, any insolvency event, any court order, any event HSBC deems material |
| **10 — Administrator/Receiver** | HSBC can appoint an administrator or receiver at any time after enforcement — effectively taking control of the business |
| **Property schedules** | **BLANK** — no specific property listed. The lease is either in a different name, not yet completed, or covered by the general floating charge. |

### What the HSBC charge tells us

1. **The Zarkovics needed bank funding on top of their £138k directors' loan.** The bank
   loan was taken in March 2025, right around studio opening. This suggests the total
   project cost for Wandsworth exceeded what they could fund personally.

2. **Total estimated capital deployed in Wandsworth:** £138k (directors' loan as at Dec 2024)
   + HSBC facility (amount unknown but likely £50k–£150k given the asset base) = **£190k–£290k**.

3. **The demand facility structure means the Zarkovics have zero protection.** If HSBC
   gets nervous — if the studio has a bad quarter, if the Strong Pilates brand gets
   negative press, if there's another lockdown — HSBC can call in the loan immediately.

4. **The negative pledge prevents additional borrowing from other lenders.** If the
   Zarkovics need more capital (for Fulham or to cover Wandsworth losses), they can
   only go back to HSBC or inject more personal money. They cannot give security to
   anyone else.

## 4. Cross-analysis: what this means for your friend

### The "success story" is unverifiable

The Zarkovics are telling your friend they've "made a real success" of Wandsworth.
The public record shows:

- The only filed accounts cover the pre-opening period (zero revenue)
- The studio has been open ~12 months (Spring 2025 – April 2026)
- No trading accounts are publicly filed or due until ~September 2026
- The company was balance-sheet insolvent at 31 Dec 2024
- It had £159 in cash
- It was funded by £138k of the couple's own money plus a bank loan

**There is exactly zero public evidence of profitability.**

### The incentive structure is not neutral

The Zarkovics have strong incentives to present the franchise positively to prospective new franchisees:

1. **Financial interest:** They have £138k+ of personal money and a bank loan in Wandsworth.
   A growing, healthy-looking Strong Pilates UK network raises the perceived value of
   their own franchise. Studio closures or negative stories would damage it.

2. **Possible referral fees:** Many franchise systems pay existing franchisees a referral
   fee (£1k–£5k) for introducing new franchisees who go on to sign. Whether Strong
   Pilates does this is unknown but is industry-standard.

3. **Second-studio validation:** The Zarkovics are about to open Fulham. The more
   successful and popular the brand appears, the better their Fulham launch goes.
   Having a friend-of-a-friend open Kingston simultaneously would be helpful marketing
   for the brand in SW London.

None of this means they're lying. It means their perspective is not disinterested.

### The expansion timing is a yellow flag

The Zarkovics are opening a second studio (Fulham, May 2026) approximately 12–14
months after their first studio (Wandsworth, Spring 2025) opened. They signed the
Fulham franchise agreement and incorporated the entity in July 2024 — i.e. **before
Wandsworth had even opened**.

This is either:
- **(a)** Genuine confidence from strong early trading in Wandsworth — they saw month-1-6
  revenue exceeding expectations and decided to expand.
- **(b)** Classic multi-unit franchise over-commitment — signing for a second unit based on
  the excitement of the first, before the full cost picture (seasonality, churn, equipment
  maintenance, instructor turnover, marketing ramp-down) has had time to emerge.

Reading (a) is more charitable. Reading (b) is more historically common in franchising.
Your friend cannot tell which without seeing Wandsworth's monthly P&L.

success show the spreadsheet.
