# Strong Pilates — Franchise Research

> Research compiled April 2026 for due diligence on a UK franchise investment.
> Every claim is sourced. Where data is inferred or estimated, this is stated explicitly.

## 1. The brand

**Founded:** Australia, 2019, by Michael Ramsay (CEO) and Mark Armstrong.
([Welltodo](https://www.welltodoglobal.com/post/michael-ramsay-co-founder-and-ceo-of-strong-pilates/), [Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/person/mark-armstrong-48ec))

**Concept:** A 45–50 minute "Pilates inspired, cardio infused" full-body class blending Pilates principles, resistance training and cardio HIIT, performed on patented **Rowformer** and **Bikeformer** beds (a Reformer with rowing / cycling functionality bolted on). Programmes follow 12-week movement cycles. Sessions are pre-set, choreographed, fitness-driven rather than wellness/clinical.
([Wimbledon Quarter](https://wimbledonquarter.com/space/strong-pilates-2/), [About Time Magazine](https://www.abouttimemagazine.co.uk/style/about-time-you-discovered-strong-pilates/))

**Current global scale (early 2026):** 100+ open studios across ~14 countries; 155+ additional studios in pipeline; ~37,000 active members globally; ~1,000 academy-trained instructors; **~US$50m annual network revenue** (i.e. system-wide top-line, *not* franchisor revenue).
([Authority Magazine, Mar 2026, Mark Armstrong interview](https://medium.com/authority-magazine/mark-armstrong-of-strong-pilates-five-things-i-wish-i-knew-before-opening-a-franchise-136c52820888))

> **Implied average unit revenue: $50m / 100 studios ≈ $500k per studio per year ≈ £395k.** This is the *network mean*, dragged up by mature Australian sites that have been open 4–5 years and dragged down by year-1 studios; a brand-new UK studio would normally trail this figure substantially in years 1–2. This is one of the most useful single benchmarks in this whole pack.

## 2. UK structure & leadership — and a major red flag

The UK was launched as a Master Franchise in **August 2022**, acquired by **John "Hasto" Hastings**, a former Australian Test cricketer who had previously been the very first STRONG franchisee in Australia (Mornington, opened early 2020). Hastings relocated to London as Managing Director of Strong Pilates UK. He is backed by Australian investor **Matt Berriman**.
([Sports Gazette](https://sportsgazette.co.uk/meet-john-the-duke-hastings-the-former-aussie-cricketer-turned-pilates-pioneer/), [Business Leader](https://www.businessleader.co.uk/former-australian-cricketer-brings-innovative-fitness-franchise-to-uk/))

Stuart Broad (English cricketer) was added as **brand ambassador** (not investor / not master franchisee) in 2025.
([Franchising.com, Feb 2025](https://www.franchising.com/news/20250225_strong_pilates_unveils_50studio_uk_expansion_with_english_cricket_legend_st.html))

### The corporate restructure of December 2024 — read carefully

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 13 Sep 2022 | **Strong Pilates UK Limited** (company no. **14353169**) incorporated — the original UK master franchisor entity |
| 10 Aug 2023 | **Strong Pilates Battersea Ltd** (15063351) incorporated |
| ... | UK studio rollout begins (Islington Feb 2023, etc.) |
| 7 Dec 2024 | A director of Strong Pilates UK Limited resigns |
| **17 Dec 2024** | **Strong Pilates Battersea Ltd dissolved** — *same day* — |
| **17 Dec 2024** | **Strong Pilates Franchising UK Limited** (16140200) incorporated, registered c/o Stevens & Bolton LLP (Guildford), with **Mark Francis Armstrong** (the Australian co-founder) listed as a person associated |
| Subsequently | **Strong Pilates UK Limited put into Liquidation** (status as of search 2026) |

This is a textbook **phoenix restructure**: the old master franchisor entity is being wound up; a new "Franchising" SPV controlled directly by the Australian founder side has replaced it; the Battersea studio entity was killed on the same day the new vehicle was set up. The exact mechanics — whether this was a planned consensual restructure, a creditor-driven event, or a dispute between Hastings and the Aussie HQ — are not fully visible without paying for the liquidator's report. **But the existence of the restructure itself is unambiguous and is the single most important fact in this report.** Anyone signing a UK franchise agreement after Dec 2024 is contracting with an entity that is barely a year old and has no audited accounts.
([Companies House: Strong Pilates UK Ltd](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/14353169), [Companies House: Strong Pilates Battersea Ltd](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/15063351), [Manchester News on Strong Pilates Franchising UK Ltd](https://manchesternews.com/strong-pilates-franchising-uk-limited/))

### UK studio footprint as of April 2026

From the public locations list and ClassPass / Wellhub listings, sites that appear to be open or imminent:
- **London:** Islington (the original, opened Feb 2023), Warren Street, Fulham, Wimbledon (mid-2025), Ealing, Battersea (status unclear post-dissolution of the entity), and historically-announced flagships in Vauxhall and Hammersmith (status unclear)
- **Greater London / commuter:** St Albans, Harpenden
- **Regional UK:** Birmingham Central, Leeds Central, Trent Bridge (Nottingham)
- **Ireland (separate jurisdiction):** Dublin, Stillorgan, Galway

So the *active* UK estate is roughly **10–12 sites** despite an originally announced target of 50 sites in 3 years and "20+ locations reserved" as of late 2023. The UK rollout is running materially behind plan.
([Locations page on strongpilates.co.uk](https://strongpilates.co.uk/locations/), [Bdaily Oct 2023](https://bdaily.co.uk/articles/2023/10/23/former-australian-cricketer-targets-eleven-new-uk-fitness-studios-by-march-on-route-to-century-as-network-sales-surpass-10-million))

Network sales of "the UK network surpassing £10m" was reported in October 2023, but the journalistic phrasing in those articles is loose — it is **not clear whether £10m refers to UK or global** network sales at that time. Given that global revenue was ~$50m in early 2026, and the UK had only 3–4 stores in late 2023, **it is more plausible that the £10m figure was global network sales, not UK alone.** Treat that headline with caution.

> **Headline takeaway on the cash math:** the £250k your friend is putting down would be his *equity contribution*. The total project cost is comfortably **above £400k and could exceed £600k** once fit-out, equipment, working capital and pre-opening marketing are added. He will therefore need to **borrow at least £150–350k** on top of his £250k — almost certainly secured against the lease and personally guaranteed. In a franchise failure scenario the personal guarantees survive the limited company.

## 4. Independent UK criticism of the model

The **Pilates Consultant** (a UK industry advisor) published a detailed critique of foreign reformer franchises including Strong Pilates. The most material points:

1. The model requires *large* spaces and a *fixed* fleet of 15+ very long, wide Rowformer/Bikeformer beds. This means high rent and high capex baked in *before* the studio knows whether the local market wants this format.
2. **Pre-set, choreographed class formats** strip the franchisee of any ability to differentiate, run privates, do clinical work, retrain instructors, or pivot programming if the format underperforms.
3. **Site selection in the UK is ruthless.** UK boutique fitness "is a postcode game". A studio placed even 200 metres off-pitch suffers chronic underutilisation. Foreign franchisors imported from Australia rarely understand the micro-geography.
4. **Instructor shortage.** Reformer-trained instructors are scarce in the UK and prefer to work independently. Strong relies on its own academy-trained instructors which adds onboarding cost and time-to-open delay.
5. UK boutique pilates studios (800–2,000 sq ft) **typically do £200k–£400k turnover with net margins ranging from a loss to 20%**. Strong Pilates studios are at the larger end of that band so a healthy unit might do £350k–£600k — but that wider top end comes with much higher fixed cost so the *downside* is also worse.

([pilates-consultant.co.uk](https://www.pilates-consultant.co.uk/blog/why-uk-investors-struggle-to-see-value-in-foreign-pilates-franchise-models))

The founder Mark Armstrong's own Authority Magazine interview (March 2026) is candid: *"I didn't really know what I was doing. We made so many mistakes. But we got up every day and took the 5am class in rain, hail or shine, and eventually we broke through."* He also says he **"can tell within 30 seconds in a franchisee interview when they don't have the belief to leave the 9-to-5 they clearly loathe"**. That is a human, useful quote — but it also tells us that the franchisor is selecting for *psychological commitment*, not financial sophistication, and is well aware that franchisee burnout/failure happens.
([Authority Magazine](https://medium.com/authority-magazine/mark-armstrong-of-strong-pilates-five-things-i-wish-i-knew-before-opening-a-franchise-136c52820888))

## 5. Pricing & class economics observed at UK studios

Verified data points (April 2026):

- **Strong Pilates Islington**: Intro offer **£49 for 10 days unlimited**. ([wheree.com listing](https://strong-pilates-islington.wheree.com/))
- Class duration: 45–50 minutes
- Mandatory induction (15-min arrival before first ever class) — slightly raises customer-acquisition friction
- Grip socks required (small ancillary revenue line)
- Format positioned squarely against Reformcycle, Define London, Heartcore, Reformology in the SW London / Zone 2-3 affluent boutique market

Standard market pricing for boutique reformer pilates in inner-London zones 2-3 (representative range, gathered across Heartcore, Ten Health, Define, Reformcycle, Frame, etc.):

- Drop-in / single class: **£25–£35**
- 5-class pack: **£120–£160** (£24–£32/class)
- 10-class pack: **£220–£300** (£22–£30/class)
- Monthly unlimited: **£165–£230**
- ClassPass / Wellhub credit price (off-peak fill): equivalent of **£10–£18/class** (after the platform's cut)

For a Kingston suburban studio you should sit at the **lower-mid end** of these bands (drop-in £22–£28; unlimited £150–£185). Anything more expensive than that gets squeezed by the existing local independents (Klass, All Things Pilates, The Pilates Lab in nearby Surbiton, Kingston Physiotherapy, Pexystudio, Core Connect, plus David Lloyd Kingston and Virgin Active in the surrounding catchment).

## 6. Reputation, reviews and chatter

- ClassPass listings of UK studios (Islington, Wimbledon) generally show *positive* reviews — Strong is well-regarded as a *workout product*. The complaint is rarely the class itself, almost always pricing, intensity vs expectation, or scheduling.
- The brand attracts a fitness-first, results-driven customer (closer to F45 / Barry's Bootcamp client) rather than the wellness/restorative pilates client.
- No UK Trustpilot footprint of significance.
- No formal regulatory complaints uncovered.
- The Companies House record (Strong Pilates UK Ltd in liquidation) is the single material adverse signal.

## 7. What we still don't know — evidence gaps

A serious due diligence pack must also flag the questions that *cannot* be answered from the public record. Your friend should refuse to sign anything until he has paper answers to all of these:

1. **Why is Strong Pilates UK Limited in liquidation? What did the liquidator's Statement of Affairs disclose? Were creditors (including franchisees) paid in full?** (Available for ~£15 from Companies House for a fee — *get it*.)
2. **Is the new Strong Pilates Franchising UK Limited entity the legal counterparty to the new franchise agreement, or is the contract back-to-back with the Australian parent?** Different jurisdictions, different remedies in a dispute.
3. **What is the actual revenue, EBITDA and net profit of Strong Pilates Wimbledon (and any other open UK studio more than 12 months old)?** Almost no UK Strong studio has filed accounts yet, because they are too young — but Item 19-style FPRs (financial performance representations) should be requested in writing.
4. **What % of UK franchisees have ever exited / failed / been terminated**, since launch in 2022?
5. **Royalty + brand fund stack:** is it really only 8% + 2%? Or are there hidden tech fees, training fees, music licence pass-throughs, software seat fees, mandatory equipment upgrade contributions etc?
6. **Territory exclusivity** — what radius does the Kingston franchise exclude other Strong studios from? Could HQ open a second London-side franchise next door later?
7. **Termination clauses** — under what conditions can HQ pull the franchise? Are there minimum performance standards (typically yes)?
8. **Lease term vs franchise term** — if the franchise is 7 years and the lease is 15 years, the franchisee carries 8 years of lease liability with no brand. The lease must be no longer than the franchise plus a controlled break.
9. **Personal guarantees** — to what extent are PGs required on the lease, the equipment finance, and any HQ-arranged loan?
10. **The UK master franchise itself** — is John Hastings still UK MD? After the December 2024 events, is the UK still being run from London or remotely from Sydney?

These ten gaps are exactly the questions that, if asked in writing to the franchisor and met with vague answers, become *the reason not to invest*.
